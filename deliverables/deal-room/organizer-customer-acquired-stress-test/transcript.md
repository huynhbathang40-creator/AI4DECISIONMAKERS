# Deal Room: Largest organizer customer acquired by a Freeman-aligned company

**Question:** GES's largest show organizer customer has just been bought by a company that mostly uses Freeman. How does that change our acquisition priorities for the next 18 months?

**Date:** 2026-11-17
**Nature:** Hypothetical stress test. No real organizer is named as the buyer or the customer.

## Panel
- `corp-dev-lead` (chair of record): owns the add-on pipeline and must re-rank it.
- `show-organizer-relations-expert`: knows how much revenue is at risk and how organizers behave after ownership changes.
- `exhibitions-gsc-expert`: knows what losing the shows does to GES operations, labor and cities.
- `chief-strategy-officer`: decides whether to reduce reliance on organizers or double down on them.
- `pe-sponsor-partner`: covers the effect on Truelink's returns, leverage and exit story.
- `event-tech-registration-expert`: covers technology and data as a way to keep organizers from switching contractors, and as a diversification option.

## Round 1 — Opening positions

### corp-dev-lead
**Position: re-rank the pipeline now, but don't freeze it.** The test for every add-on over the next 18 months changes from "does this add capability?" to "does this reduce dependence on a single organizer and make GES harder to replace?"
1. **Pause or re-price** any exhibitions/GSC target whose case relies on volume from this customer's shows or cities.
2. **Move up** targets that bring organizer and association relationships GES does not serve today (CSG-type association marketing, regional contractors with their own organizer base).
3. **Move up** services organizers and exhibitors buy separately from the GSC contract (housing, registration and data, venue-exclusive power). That revenue can survive if the GSC contract is lost.

[Assumption] The acquirer moves shows to its main contractor at renewal, not immediately. That gives us time, but we don't know how much. [Assumption] The exposure is large enough that a future buyer will treat it as concentration risk; no revenue split is available.

**Top risk: buying into the problem.** That means an add-on whose earnings depend on the same at-risk shows, or a third or fourth integration on a team that also has to fight to keep this account. GES has already done two add-ons under Truelink [Fact – profile].

**→ show-organizer-relations-expert:** Which of this customer's contracts have change-of-control or termination-for-convenience clauses, and when does each major show renew? That tells me whether the next 18 months are defensive or still offensive.

### show-organizer-relations-expert
**Position:** Losing this customer is not certain; it is a slow risk that plays out show by show over 12–36 months. For the next 18 months the top priority is keeping the account, and that shapes M&A. Put add-ons that lower organizer concentration ahead of any deal that adds more GSC show volume, and check every target's customer list for links to this buyer.
- [Assumption] Contracts usually run 3–5 years. A new owner more often consolidates contractors at the next RFP than terminates mid-term. Change-of-control and assignment clauses decide how fast this hits.
- [Assumption] The buyer will want to standardize on Freeman. Shows run by teams with strong relationships with us, or tied to a specific venue or city, have the best chance of staying. Rank them show by show.
- [Estimate] If this customer is above roughly 15–20% of Exhibitions profit, it is concentration that a future buyer will mark down whatever happens to the contracts.

Pipeline: (1) raise targets not dependent on any single organizer: corporate and brand exhibits, associations, tech and data the organizer would have to rip out to leave; (2) lower or re-check GSC add-ons whose top customers overlap with the buyer's portfolio.

**Top risk:** spending on deals before we know the renewal calendar.

**→ exhibitions-gsc-expert:** If these shows move to Freeman at renewal, which cities and venues lose enough volume that local labor, warehouse and equipment become uneconomic? Do we backfill through acquisitions or shrink capacity?

### exhibitions-gsc-expert
**Position:** Pause GSC add-ons that add capacity until we know which shows leave and when. Then put three things ahead of agency and creative add-ons: regional GSCs with show lists spread across many independent organizers and associations, targets in the cities where the at-risk shows are concentrated, and services exhibitors buy whichever contractor the organizer appoints.

**Why:** Losing these shows leaves fixed costs behind: warehouse space, rental inventory and crews, which are slow to cut under union rules [Assumption]. Most of the margin goes with the shows, because exhibitor orders and material handling follow the official contractor [Assumption]. The buyer will probably move shows to Freeman at each renewal [Assumption]. The real 18-month exposure is the set of shows renewing in that window.

**Top risk:** buying a regional GSC to fill the gap and inheriting the same weakness: concentration in one organizer, renewals inside 12 months, or ageing inventory. The worst case is that the buyer is already one of its customers.

**→ show-organizer-relations-expert:** One master agreement or show-by-show contracts? Change-of-control clause? Which shows renew in the next 18 months?

### chief-strategy-officer
**Position: shift weight toward buyers who don't depend on organizers. No panic buy to win back GSC volume.**
1. **Move up: brand, exhibitor and corporate-event demand.** That means Spiro/2Heads-type agencies, exhibit rental and reusable-system builders, and sustainability services. These sell to exhibitors and brands, who keep buying whoever holds the GSC contract [Assumption].
2. **Hold, but test: organizer-facing technology and data** (registration, housing, attendee data). Only buy if it demonstrably makes organizers stickier; "higher multiple" alone is not a reason.
3. **Move down: regional GSC consolidation in the most exposed cities.** It brings labor and pension exposure just as our utilisation falls [Estimate].

Organizer volume is won through contracts, not acquisitions. A rival contractor's contracts carry the same change-of-control risk that is hurting us now.

**Top risk:** We may underestimate how far the damage spreads. If onPeak, SHOWTECH and Visit are sold to these shows as a bundle with the GSC contract, they leave too.

**→ show-organizer-relations-expert:** Which services on the lost shows are contracted separately from the GSC agreement, and how much term is left? That decides whether organizer tech is a defense or a diversification bet.

### pe-sponsor-partner
**View:** This changes priority order more than the overall plan. For the next 18 months the add-on program should make earnings hard to take away, not buy back lost revenue at any price. Truelink bought GES around late 2024 [Fact – profile], so the next 18 months, to about mid-2028, are roughly years 2–4 of the hold [Assumption]. That is when the exit story gets written. A buyer will ask: "Did they lose their biggest customer to Freeman?"
1. **Move up:** add-ons whose revenue doesn't depend on organizer contracts: brand-direct experiential, recurring tech and data, association marketing [Estimate: supports a re-rating].
2. **Move down:** large GSC deals bought to backfill trade-show earnings.
3. **Hold back** a reserve of debt capacity instead of spending it all on M&A.

**Top risk:** leverage moves before the customer actually leaves. EBITDA runs off, debt stays, covenant headroom shrinks just when we want to fund add-ons [Assumption: no debt data]. `cfo-finance-partner` should model it first.

**→ show-organizer-relations-expert:** Contract expiry, change-of-control or termination-for-convenience clause, and how much revenue leaves in year 1 versus at renewal? Six months of runway or 30 changes the M&A pace completely.

### event-tech-registration-expert
**Position:** Technology won't win this customer back. Buy software that makes GES harder to replace for the *other* organizers and that earns revenue whichever contractor is on the floor. Move a contractor-neutral data or exhibitor-tech asset up the pipeline, but only one that passes strict data-rights checks.
- Registration is usually bought separately from the GSC contract, so Visit may hold the business longer than GES Exhibitions [Assumption].
- Organizers usually own attendee data, so Visit's data is not a moat [Fact – role knowledge; Assumption that this contract follows the norm].
- What keeps organizers from switching is workflow: an exhibitor portal, lead retrieval and ordering connected to Visit [Estimate].
- Software sold whatever the contractor (lead retrieval, matchmaking, exhibitor portals) protects revenue and helps the exit story. Cvent will bid for the same assets.

**Top risk:** overpaying for "sticky" software whose revenue is really per-event onsite services, or whose customer contracts do not let GES use the data across units.

**→ show-organizer-relations-expert:** Are Visit and the GSC contract bundled for this customer, or separate with different renewal dates and change-of-control terms?
